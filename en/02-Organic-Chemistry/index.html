🧬 Organic Chemistry: The Architecture of Carbon

More than twenty million compounds are known on Earth, and the vast majority of them contain carbon. A carbon atom has four "ports," letting it link into chains, close into rings, and assemble endlessly varied three-dimensional shapes—organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon and the architecture of molecules: the carbon skeleton is the building's frame, and the functional groups are the tenants that give it character. In 4 chapters, this branch takes you from "reading a molecule" to "reading a reaction step."

4 chapters · about 245 minutes Prerequisites: 00 Foundations of Chemistry (especially Chapter 2, Chemical Bonding) Interactive animations ×8 20 end-of-chapter self-test questions

🎯 What you will learn in this branch

  • Chapter 1 · Carbon Skeletons and Functional Groups—how carbon manages to build twenty million molecules; the four great families of alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and arenes; meet the functional-group "lead actors" that determine properties, and learn to count isomers and look up basic IUPAC names.
  • Chapter 2 · Stereochemistry—molecules are three-dimensional objects: tetrahedral geometry, conformational rotation, cis-trans isomerism, and enantiomers that mirror each other like left and right hands yet cannot be superimposed (chirality).
  • Chapter 3 · Introduction to Reaction Mechanisms—learn the chemist's "curved-arrow language" and follow three classic scripts: free-radical chain substitution, nucleophilic substitution (SN1/SN2), and electrophilic addition (Markovnikov's rule).
  • Chapter 4 · A Tour of Functional-Group Reactions—string the signature reactions of six functional-group families into a single transformation map: from margarine and drunk-driving breath tests to fruity esters and soap.

After completing this branch, you will be able to: read the organic molecules on food and medicine labels, tell whether a molecule is chiral, predict the products of simple reactions, and lay the groundwork for 05 Biochemistry, 06 Polymer Chemistry, and 10 Medicinal Chemistry.

🧭 Prerequisites and study path

This branch assumes you have completed 00 Foundations of Chemistry, especially Chapter 2, "Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure": covalent bonds, σ/π bonds, electronegativity, and polarity come up again and again in this branch. If "polar covalent bond" has grown rusty, it is worth going back to review §2 before setting out.

The recommended order is simply the chapter order: skeletons and functional groups (getting to know molecules) → stereochemistry (their 3D shapes) → reaction mechanisms (how molecules change) → the reaction tour (integrated practice). The first three chapters are "language lessons"; Chapter 4 is "reading comprehension."

🗺️ Sub-discipline map (signposts beyond this branch)

  • Stereochemistry—devoted to the three-dimensional arrangement and handedness of molecules; the guardian angel of medicinal chemistry.
  • Reaction mechanisms—drawing every electron shift explicitly; the "grammar book" of organic chemistry.
  • Natural product chemistry—isolating and identifying marvelous molecules such as artemisinin and paclitaxel from plants and microorganisms.
  • Organometallic chemistry—compounds of carbon and metals (such as Grignard reagents) that hand synthetic chemists a super toolbox.
  • Organic synthesis—designing routes like a building project, constructing complex molecules from simple feedstocks (a regular on the Nobel stage).
  • Supramolecular chemistry—the study of "handshakes between molecules": hydrogen bonds, host–guest recognition, molecular machines.
