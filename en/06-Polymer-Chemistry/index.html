🧶 Polymer Chemistry: From Small-Molecule Monomers to Giant Chain Molecules

A single ethylene molecule weighs just 28 atomic mass units and, at room temperature, is a gas that drifts away in an instant; string tens of thousands of them together head to tail, and you get a plastic pail that can carry a bucketful of water. This branch turns on a single key: join small molecules (monomers) into giant chain molecules (polymers), and properties take a qualitative leap — soft, tough, spinnable into fibers, formable into films. One polymerization reaction props up plastics, rubber, fibers, and half of the modern materials world.

🎯 What You Will Learn in This Branch

The central question of polymer chemistry is: how monomers connect, what kinds of chains they build, and how those chains pack into a block of material. This chapter walks you along the "molecule → chain → network → material" route:

  • What a polymer is — degree of polymerization, average molecular weight, and molecular weight distribution; why "a polymer has no single molecular weight".
  • The two major polymerization routes — addition polymerization (a free-radical chain process: initiation → propagation → termination; ethylene → polyethylene) and condensation polymerization (stepwise linking that squeezes out small-molecule by-products; nylon-66 and PET).
  • Chain shape determines character — linear, branched, and cross-linked networks; how vulcanization turns sticky raw rubber into the king of elasticity.
  • Thermoplastic vs thermoset — why a plastic bottle can be melted down and remade, while a shattered Bakelite socket is beyond saving.
  • A quick tour of the material families — PE, PP, PVC, PS, PET, nylon, rubber, and cellulose; one table for recognizing recycling codes 1–7.

After this chapter, you will be able to: read the recycling triangle stamped on the bottom of plastic products, work out how common monomers polymerize, use "chain structure" to explain why a plastic is soft or hard and whether it can be recycled, and lay the foundation for 07 Materials Chemistry and deeper study of polymers.

🗺️ Sub-branch Map (Signposts Beyond This Chapter)

  • Polymerization reaction engineering — scaling polymerization from the beaker to the reactor: heat transfer, mass transfer, kinetics, and process control (high-pressure, emulsion, and continuous polymerization).
  • Polymer physics — how chains move, crystallize, and entangle; the molecular roots of the glass transition temperature, the melting point, and mechanical properties.
  • Functional polymers — conducting polymers, ion-exchange resins, photosensitive and adsorbent materials: putting polymers "to work" instead of merely "holding structure".
  • Biomedical polymers — biodegradable sutures, sustained-release drug carriers, and artificial-organ materials, with biocompatibility as the core challenge.
  • Polymer processing — extrusion, injection molding, film blowing, and fiber spinning: how chain molecules get "arranged" by machines into bottles, films, and fibers.
  • Fine and specialty polymers — high-value-added varieties such as heat-resistant polyimides, carbon-fiber precursors, and hyperbranched polymers.
