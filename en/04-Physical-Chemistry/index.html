Branch 04 · The Five Pillars of Chemistry

⚡ Physical Chemistry: The Physics Behind Change

Why does water freeze at exactly 0 °C? Iron rusts while gold stays untarnished for a thousand years — who made that rule? Refrigeration keeps food fresh, but what exactly does the low temperature "freeze"? These questions come down to three: why does a change happen? how fast does it happen? and where does it finally lead? Chemists hand these three questions over to physics and mathematics to answer — and that is physical chemistry. It is chemistry's "rulebook": thermodynamics decides direction and extent, kinetics measures speed, electrochemistry exchanges energy, and quantum chemistry dives into the microscopic world to explain why chemical bonds exist. Across 4 chapters, this branch helps you build this quantitative way of thinking.

4 chapters · about 240 minutes (about 4 hours) Prerequisites: all of 00 Foundations + at least 1 chapter each of 01/02 Interactive animations ×5 End-of-chapter self-test: 20 questions ⚠️ Fairly calculation-heavy — have pen and paper ready

🎯 What You Will Learn in This Branch

  • Chapter 1 · Chemical Thermodynamics — the "accounting" of energy and direction: state functions, the first law of thermodynamics, enthalpy and heat of reaction, Hess's law, and entropy, finally converging on the spontaneity criterion ΔG = ΔH − TΔS — why is ice melting at room temperature "spontaneous" and yet endothermic?
  • Chapter 2 · Chemical Kinetics — the speedometer of change: rate laws and reaction order (order is determined by experiment), activation energy and the Arrhenius equation, why heating speeds reactions up exponentially, and how catalysts "take a shortcut" without changing the destination.
  • Chapter 3 · Electrochemistry — the "exchange office" between chemical energy and electrical energy: the copper-zinc voltaic cell and the salt bridge, the ordering of electrode potentials, the Nernst equation, electrolysis and Faraday's law, and the "rocking chair" story inside the lithium-ion battery.
  • Chapter 4 · Quantum Chemistry and Molecular Structure — the "constitution" of the microscopic world: quantum numbers and orbital shapes, the Aufbau principle and the quantum roots of the periodic table, how molecular orbital theory explains the paramagnetism of O₂, and hybrid orbitals and molecular shape.

After finishing this branch, you will be able to: judge how a reaction's spontaneity depends on temperature, estimate the effect of heating on rate, read the quantitative ledger of batteries and electrolysis, and understand why the periodic table looks the way it does — laying solid groundwork for branches such as 07 Materials Chemistry and 09 Theoretical and Computational Chemistry.

🧭 Prerequisites and Study Path

This branch assumes you have completed all chapters of 00 Foundations, especially moles and stoichiometry — the amount of substance n runs through every formula in this branch like the air you breathe; and that you have read at least 1 chapter each of 01 Inorganic Chemistry and 02 Organic Chemistry: the worked examples here draw on old friends such as redox and combustion reactions.

On the math side, you need only arithmetic, scientific notation, and a first acquaintance with exponents and logarithms (lg and e^x appear only in kinetics). Note: this is the most calculation-heavy branch in the whole book. Work through every example with your own pen — physical chemistry is understood by calculating, not by reading.

The recommended order is the chapter order: thermodynamics (can it happen) → kinetics (how fast) → electrochemistry (energy exchange) → quantum chemistry (microscopic foundations). The first two chapters are the backbone of the branch; the last two are the two wings on that backbone.

🗺️ Subfield Map (Signposts Beyond This Branch)

  • Chemical thermodynamics — uses energy and entropy to judge the direction and extent of change; it is the "referee" of all chemistry.
  • Chemical kinetics — measures and explains how fast reactions run, from exhaust-gas cleanup to drug shelf life.
  • Electrochemistry — the science of batteries, electrolysis, corrosion, and electroplating; a core discipline of the new-energy era.
  • Quantum chemistry — applies quantum mechanics to molecules, explaining chemical bonds, orbitals, and spectra from first principles.
  • Statistical thermodynamics — builds a statistical bridge between billions of microscopic states and macroscopic temperature and pressure.
  • Colloid and surface chemistry — studies the special behavior of finely dispersed systems and interfaces; milk, paint, and catalyst supports all belong here.
